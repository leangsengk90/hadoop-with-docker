To effectively leverage your normalized data for Sale and Marketing, you need to transform raw transactions into actionable insights. Below are the Top 10 Business Analytics and Visualizations that provide a comprehensive view of business health, customer behavior, and product performance.

1. Sales & Profit Trends (Seasonality Analysis)
Why: Marketing teams need to know when to launch campaigns. Identifying peaks (e.g., holiday seasons) helps in budget allocation and inventory planning.

2. Regional Profitability Heatmap
Why: Helps Sales leaders decide where to expand or where to optimize logistics. Some regions may have high sales but low profit due to shipping costs.

3. Product Category Mix
Why: Visualizes which categories (Furniture vs. Tech vs. Office Supplies) drive the most revenue, helping focus marketing assets on the "Bread and Butter" of the business.

4. High-Margin "Star" Products
Why: Identifying the top 10 most profitable products allows Marketing to create featured promotions that maximize the bottom line rather than just revenue.

5. Customer Segment Performance
Why: Analyzes the contribution of Consumer, Corporate, and Home Office segments. This is vital for targeted digital advertising (e.g., LinkedIn for Corporate vs. Instagram for Consumers).

6. Discount vs. Profit Correlation
Why: A critical "sanity check" for Sales. High discounts increase volume but can destroy profit margins. This scatter plot reveals the "danger zone" of over-discounting.

7. Shipping Mode Efficiency
Why: Evaluates how customers prefer to receive goods. If "Same Day" is popular but unprofitable, it may require a price adjustment.

8. Customer Revenue Ranking (Pareto Analysis)
Why: Identifies your "Whales." The top 10 customers often contribute a disproportionate amount of revenue and should be prioritized for loyalty programs.

9. State-Level Performance (Sales vs. Profit)
Why: A granular view to identify "Problem States" where sales are high but profits are negative (often due to local taxes or shipping distances).

10. Average Order Value (AOV) by Segment
Why: AOV is a key KPI for growth. If the AOV for the "Consumer" segment is low, Marketing can implement "Buy One Get One" or "Bundle" offers to increase it.
